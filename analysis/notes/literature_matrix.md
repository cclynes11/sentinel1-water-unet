# Literature matrix: landscape proxies for OOD performance of SAR flood models

Verification status for each entry is in [`citation_verification.md`](citation_verification.md).
⚠️ = verified with a caveat (preprint, ambiguous, or findings not yet read in full). Zimmermann is left out because I could not identify the paper (❌).

Column "Relevance" asks one question: **what does this paper give us as a candidate proxy for where and when a SAR flood or water model will fail?**

## A. SAR flood and water segmentation

| Paper | Method | Data | OOD / uncertainty approach | Key finding | Relevance to proxies for SAR flood-model failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nemni et al. 2020** (Remote Sensing 12:2532) | Fully convolutional networks for flood segmentation | Sentinel-1 SAR, no optical bands, minimal preprocessing; UNOSAT flood masks (semi-automated plus manual cleaning) | None explicit. Evaluated across varied locations and conditions | Cuts flood-map production time by about 80% compared with semi-automated UNOSAT workflows while performing well across varied events | Operational baseline: the rapid-mapping setting where a failure proxy is most needed. Labels come from earlier semi-automated mapping, so label noise is itself a confounder for "failure" |
| **Portalés-Julià et al. 2025** ⚠️ (RSE 328:114882) | U-Nets: S1-only, S2-only, and a dual-stream S1+S2 model with a modality token | S1 GRD VV/VH (S1S2Water, Kuro Siwo); S2 L1C (S1S2Water, WorldFloods) | Cross-dataset and cross-modality comparison (from the title and model card; full text not yet read) | **Placeholder. Read the full text.** The title points to how flood models behave across benchmark datasets and modalities | Cross-dataset transfer is a form of distribution shift. Likely evidence that benchmark and label definitions drive apparent performance gaps. Trained weights are public, so these models are a candidate second model alongside the Wagner U-Net |
| **Thasma et al. 2026** ⚠️ (arXiv:2606.29134) | Same fusion design across four backbones (CNN U-Net, ImageNet U-Net, TerraMind ViT, DINOv3 ViT); inputs SAR-only, SAR+DEM, SAR+AlphaEarth | ImpactMesh-Flood, CONUS subset; event-stratified split (held-out test events Hurricane Florence and Louisiana) | Per-event held-out evaluation; three seeds | Both land-context priors beat SAR-only. AlphaEarth is best on the harder Florence event, DEM is competitive on Louisiana. Cross-event differences track **flood-class prevalence** and **similarity to the training distribution** | Direct support for two proxies: (1) flood-class prevalence per tile or event, and (2) training-to-test similarity in a land-context embedding (AlphaEarth). DEM-derived terrain is also a candidate proxy |
| **Kolluru et al. 2026** ⚠️ (arXiv:2606.07780) | Prithvi-EO-2.0 geospatial foundation model run on unseen events; dual-reference validation; failure-mode catalogue | 19 flood events, 2017–2025, six continents, eight climate zones, six flood mechanisms; two independent reference products (sensor to be confirmed) | Explicit OOD evaluation on unseen events, stratified by land cover and flood type | Cropland agreement highest (IoU about 0.52), riverine floods best (F1 about 0.69); **tree cover and built-up near zero (IoU about 0.04)** whatever the flood mechanism; some apparent error comes from the two reference products disagreeing; 23 failure modes catalogued | Strongest direct evidence that **land cover (tree cover, built-up)** and **flood type** predict failure. Also a caution: reference-label disagreement inflates "failure", so the analysis must control for label source |
| **Wagner et al. 2024** (Remote Sensing 16:1056) | U-Net (34.6 M parameters), VV+VH σ⁰ in dB scaled to 8 bits | 59,387 Sentinel-1 patches, Amazon basin (2021–22, S1A), high-resolution reference water masks | None. In-domain validation only (F1 = 0.93) | Accurate in-domain mapping of Rio Negro contraction; known errors: radar shadow looks like water, narrow rivers (1–2 px) missed, flooded forest not mapped, convective rain cells cause omissions | **The model under study.** Its documented failure modes (terrain shadow, flooded vegetation, narrow channels, rain cells) map directly onto landscape proxies (slope and shadow, tree cover, channel width, weather). Training domain is a single biome, so most of the world is OOD |
| **Zhao et al. 2024, UrbanSARFloods** (CVPRW 2024) | CNN segmentation baselines; weighted cross-entropy; transfer learning from pretrained weights | Sentinel-1 SLC: pre- and co-event intensity plus InSAR coherence, VV/VH; 8,879 chips (512×512); 18 events; 5 continents; 20 land-cover classes | None explicit. Class imbalance and small training sets examined | Weighted cross-entropy and pretrained transfer fall short on imbalanced data; **urban flood is the hardest class** | Primary dataset. Its non-flood / open flood / urban flood labels allow a clean test of "built-up fraction → failure". Coherence channels give a physically motivated contrast to intensity-only models |

## B. OOD detection, uncertainty, and applicability (methodological)

| Paper | Method | Data | OOD / uncertainty approach | Key finding | Relevance to proxies for SAR flood-model failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Meyer & Pebesma 2021** (MEE 12:1620) | Dissimilarity index (DI): importance-weighted minimum distance to training data in predictor space, normalised; **area of applicability (AOA)** = DI below a threshold (suggested: 0.95 quantile of the DI in the training data) | Simulated and real spatial prediction (ecology / environmental ML) | Feature-space distance to training data | Inside the AOA, prediction error matches cross-validation error; outside it, cross-validation error does not transfer | Template for a **landscape AOA**: compute DI over landscape covariates (land cover fractions, HAND, slope, permanent water, climate zone) instead of raw pixels, then test whether DI predicts per-tile IoU drop |
| **Le Bellier & Audebert 2024** (CVPRW 2024) | ODEED: diffusion-model reconstruction error through a probability-flow ODE, used as an unsupervised plausibility score | SpaceNet 8 (geographic shift; pre/post-flood, flooded/non-flooded) | Unsupervised, label-free OOD scoring | ODEED beats other diffusion-based and discriminative baselines, especially in the harder **near-OOD flood** setting | Image-space OOD score to compare against landscape proxies. Flood imagery is "near OOD", so generic detectors may struggle. Optical (SpaceNet 8), not SAR: transfer to SAR is untested |
| **Hendrycks & Gimpel 2017** (ICLR) | Maximum softmax probability (MSP) | Vision, NLP and speech benchmarks | Model-confidence baseline for misclassification and OOD detection | Correct predictions have higher MSP than errors or OOD inputs; proposes standard evaluation (AUROC, AUPR) | **Baseline proxy** that every landscape proxy must beat: per-pixel MSP or entropy averaged to the tile |
| **Sun et al. 2022** ⚠️ (ICML; kNN) | Distance to the k-th nearest training embedding (non-parametric, normalised features) | CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-1k | Feature-space OOD score | kNN distance beats parametric (Mahalanobis) scores without distribution assumptions; can be combined with ReAct (Sun et al. 2021) | **Embedding-distance proxy.** Can use the U-Net bottleneck or a foundation-model embedding (cf. AlphaEarth in Thasma 2026). The deep-learning counterpart of the Meyer & Pebesma DI |
| **Ovadia et al. 2019** (NeurIPS) | Large benchmark of uncertainty methods (temperature scaling, MC dropout, ensembles, SVI, last-layer methods) under increasing shift | Image, text, ads and genomics; corrupted and shifted test sets | Calibration and accuracy as a function of shift intensity | Calibration degrades with shift; post-hoc temperature scaling is not enough; **deep ensembles are most robust** | Justifies a small ensemble (or MC dropout) as the strong model-based proxy baseline; reliability diagrams per landscape stratum |
| **Guérin et al. 2023** (AAAI) | Reframes runtime monitoring as **out-of-model-scope (OMS)** detection: flag incorrect predictions, not just OOD inputs | Image classification benchmarks | Evaluates monitors on error detection, not on in/out-of-distribution separation | Good OOD scores can give a false sense of safety; OOD benchmarks do not identify the best error monitor; removing bad training samples helps | **Defines the evaluation target:** a proxy should be judged by how well it predicts *IoU loss* (risk-coverage / AURC), not by whether it separates training-event and test-event tiles |
| **de Jorge et al. 2023** (CVPR) | Systematic study of modern segmenters (CNN and transformer) | Urban-scene segmentation under natural shift (unseen geography, weather) | Robustness, calibration, misclassification detection, OOD detection | Newer models are more robust but **not** better at uncertainty estimation; better calibration also helps other uncertainty metrics | Segmentation-specific evaluation protocol (pixel-level plus aggregate). Warns against assuming a stronger backbone gives a more trustworthy failure signal |
| **Koh et al. 2021, WILDS** (ICML) | Curated benchmark of 10 real-world shift datasets, including satellite (FMoW, PovertyMap) | Domain generalisation and subpopulation shift | Standardised in-distribution vs OOD splits | Large in-distribution → OOD gaps; existing robustness methods do not close them | Framing and terminology (domain vs subpopulation shift); argues for **reporting the in-distribution vs OOD gap per group**, e.g. per flood event or land-cover stratum |

## C. Synthesis: candidate proxies drawn from the literature

- **Landscape and context proxies** (input-independent, available before the image arrives)
  - Tree-cover fraction and built-up fraction → Kolluru 2026, Zhao 2024, Wagner 2024 (flooded forest)
  - Flood type or mechanism (riverine, coastal, pluvial, ...) → Kolluru 2026
  - Terrain: slope, HAND, radar-shadow likelihood → Wagner 2024 (shadow = false water), Thasma 2026 (DEM prior)
  - Flood-class prevalence and permanent-water fraction → Thasma 2026
  - Landscape dissimilarity to training (AOA DI on covariates) → Meyer & Pebesma 2021
- **Representation proxies**
  - kNN distance in U-Net bottleneck or foundation embedding → Sun 2022, Thasma 2026 (AlphaEarth)
  - Generative plausibility (diffusion reconstruction) → Le Bellier & Audebert 2024
- **Model-output proxies** (baselines)
  - MSP / entropy → Hendrycks & Gimpel 2017
  - Ensemble / MC-dropout disagreement → Ovadia 2019
- **Evaluation principles**
  - Score proxies on error prediction (AURC, Spearman ρ with IoU loss), not in-distribution vs OOD separation → Guérin 2023
  - Report results per group or event, not pooled → WILDS 2021, Thasma 2026
  - Control for reference-label disagreement → Kolluru 2026, Nemni 2020
